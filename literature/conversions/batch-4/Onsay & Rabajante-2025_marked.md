This work has been carried out and is currently being conducted in the poorest region of Luzon, Philippines. 
Since poverty is multifaceted and considered unmeasurable in social science, it is notoriously difficult to mea-
sure. The methods currently used to measure poverty require a significant amount of time, money, and labor. 
This poses challenges for policymakers in implementing poverty-reduction policies. Indigenous communities are 
among the most disadvantaged, vulnerable, and neglected populations in society, facing complex and diverse 
socioeconomic situations. Poverty stands as one of the oldest and most challenging social problems to have ever 
existed. Research on indigenous peoples typically takes a qualitative approach, whereas studies on poverty tend 
to be broad, making them susceptible to significant sampling errors and primarily intended for national policy- 
making.  Using  community-based  monitoring  system  (CBMS)  data,  we  achieved  a  prediction  accuracy  of 
92.60–98.00 % using Random Forest classification and reduced traditional survey and data processing costs by 
up to 70 %. The proposed model incorporates 27 socioeconomic variables and enables localized policy targeting. 
Therefore, it is crucial to assess multifaceted poverty and simulate socioeconomic conditions for each tribe to 
foster economic development. By training and testing datasets, this work proposes new metrics and illustrates the 
effectiveness  of  machine  learning  in  predicting  poverty.  Lastly,  the  results  provide  various  localities  with 
customized  policy  targeting  tools  for  poverty  alleviation.  These  techniques  can  be  replicated,  adapted,  or 
repurposed by other researchers to assist impoverished populations in improving their well-being.

Social impact

Our research focuses on measuring previously unquantifiable socie-
tal issues [1,2]. Understanding the societal implications of this research 
within economic contexts and among indigenous populations is crucial 
[3].

This study integrates econometric modeling with machine learning 
techniques to enhance the accuracy and applicability of poverty fore-
casts. Traditional poverty research in the country has primarily relied on 
classic regression analysis, which often limits predictive precision and 
policy  responsiveness.  By  contrast,  our  approach  generates  forecasts 
that can support more targeted and data-driven interventions for rural 
communities  and  marginalized  sectors  at  both  disaggregated  and  na-
tional levels. These results offer a framework that can be adapted not 
only across municipalities in the Philippines but also in similarly situ-
ated regions and developing countries worldwide.

Current poverty metrics are often outdated and dependent on costly 
surveys and censuses that require substantial resources in terms of time 
and  funding.  Therefore,  the  results  of  this  investigation  provide  an 
alternative to the conventional reliance on regression-based economet-
ric approaches that have dominated poverty research in the Philippines. 
By  incorporating  machine  learning  techniques,  the  study  introduces 
predictive  and  classification  models  that  can  uncover  complex,  non- 
linear  patterns  in  poverty  data,  offering  more  actionable  insights  for 
policymakers and development practitioners.

To enhance the accuracy and efficiency of poverty predictions, we 
have integrated econometrics and machine learning in this work. The 
resulting predictions can be employed to target policies and programs 
for  rural  sectors  and  localities  at  both  aggregated  and  disaggregated 
levels.  Furthermore,  the  government’s  current  analyses  are  primarily 
descriptive  in  nature.  By  providing  diagnostic,  predictive,  and  pre-

In the Philippines, the Bicol region stands out as the poorest region in 
Luzon,  with  Camarines  Sur  identified  as  the  poorest  province  within 
Bicol,  exhibiting  a  poverty  incidence  of  38.7 %  [1,2].  Through  the 
application of economic analyses, econometric modeling, data analytics, 
and informatics, our research project aims to capture the multidimen-
sionality  of  poverty  and  economic  conditions  from  a  microeconomic 
perspective in Goa, Camarines Sur. Given the multidimensional nature 
of  poverty,  it  is  crucial  to  measure  poverty  and  inequality  across 
cross-sectional,  repeated  cross-sectional,  and  panel  data  to  effectively 
target programs for poverty reduction and socio-economic development 
activities for the upliftment of Bicolanos [4].

Data  Analytics  enables  the  extraction  of  technical,  practical, 
computational, and real insights from vast datasets through the science 
and art of analysis. Data informatics enhances the reliability and validity 
of  analyses  through  the  integration,  application,  and  management  of 
technology, people, and procedures, aiming to measure the unmeasur-
able in the natural and social sciences [5,6].

This  research  aligns  strongly  with  the  United  Nations  Sustainable 
Development Goals, particularly Goal 1 (No Poverty) and Goal 3 (Good 
Health  and  Well-being).  By  employing  machine  learning  and  econo-
metric models to measure multidimensional poverty and predict depri-
vation at the household and community level, we contribute directly to 
SDG 1 by providing evidence-based tools to accelerate poverty eradi-
cation.  The  proposed  policy  interventions,  ranging  from  income- 
generating programs to food security and educational support, are tar-
geted  toward  the  poorest  and  most  marginalized  sectors,  including 
indigenous peoples. Moreover, by addressing maternal mortality, child 
nutrition,  and  access  to  basic  sanitation  and  healthcare  services,  the 
study also advances SDG 3.

Our localized approach ensures that no one is left behind and pro-
vides  a  scalable  model  for  achieving  these  goals  in  similarly  impov-
erished  regions  worldwide.  Recent  global  studies  also  underscore  the 
multidimensional  implications  of  poverty  alleviation.  For  instance, 
Wang et al. explore the decoupling relationship between CO₂ emissions 
and poverty alleviation in China, suggesting that it is possible to reduce 
poverty  without  increasing  environmental  burdens  [11].  Similarly, 
Zheng  et  al.  examine  the  global  carbon  impact  of  poverty  alleviation 
strategies,  highlighting  the  need  for  sustainable  models  that  balance 
development  and  environmental  responsibility  [12].  These  insights 
complement  our  localized, data-driven approach by  emphasizing that 
effective poverty targeting must also consider long-term economic and 
ecological sustainability, particularly in vulnerable regions like the Bicol 
area.

poverty alleviation programs and supports the design of data-informed 
interventions  for  economic  development,  particularly  in  marginalized 
and indigenous communities.

Methodology

We propose new methods to measure the unmeasurable multifaceted 
poverty  deprivations  and  socioeconomic  conditions.  This  includes:  a 
comprehensive statistical analysis to examine socioeconomic situations 
using diagnostic and descriptive analytics; a blend of advanced econo-
metrics and predictive analytics to gauge multidimensional poverty; and 
the incorporation of machine learning to model socioeconomic scenarios 
and prescriptive analytics to formulate policies [1,2].

happened by delving into data to identify patterns and root causes.)

the incorporation of machine learning to model socioeconomic scenarios
and prescriptive analytics to formulate policies [1,2].

Causal  inference  methods  such  as  Randomized  Control  Trials,
Instrumental Variable Regression, and Regression Discontinuity Design
enable robust estimation of intervention impacts. RCTs ensure unbiased
comparisons  through  random  assignment,  IV  regression  addresses
endogeneity by using external instruments, and RDD leverages cutoff-
based assignments to estimate causal effects. Together, these methods
strengthen the validity of poverty-related policy evaluations.

Machine learning algorithms are employed in this study to analyze
diverse data points, such as income, education, health indicators, and
demographics to predict the likelihood of individuals or communities
experiencing poverty. Using community-based datasets, the models are
trained on historical data to identify complex, non-linear relationships
that traditional methods may overlook [1,2]. Once trained, these models
can generate predictions for new, unseen populations, enabling policy-
makers to target interventions more precisely, allocate resources more
efficiently, and tailor poverty alleviation programs to specific commu-
nity needs.

While this work  uses data from the Philippines’  community-based
monitoring  system,  the  methodology  is  adaptable  to  other  regions
with  similar  local  census  data.  The  study  introduces  a  sequence  of
methods  and  techniques  that  can  be  replicated  or  modified  by  re-
searchers, planners, and development agencies globally—particularly in
low-income and developing countries. Through rigorous data process-
ing, including cleaning, filtering, and feature engineering, the resulting
insights offer benchmarks for economic analysis and poverty targeting.
To evaluate the impact of data-driven policies, the study also pro-
poses  monitoring  frameworks  using  treatment  and  control  groups,
enabling  assessments  of  causal  effects  and  changes  over  time  [8,10].
This shift from raw data to informed decision-making exemplifies how
machine learning can drive transformative change in public policy, so-
cial planning, and poverty reduction efforts worldwide.

This  study  demonstrates  how  machine  learning-generated  poverty
maps can support more efficient targeting of interventions at the local
level. By identifying areas with high poverty prevalence and populations
vulnerable to transient poverty, development efforts such as maternal
health outreach, school feeding programs, and disaster preparedness can
be  more  data-driven  and  responsive.  This  approach  reduces  mis-
allocated aid and ensures that the most at-risk households receive timely
support.  The methodology  can be  adapted by  governments  and  orga-
nizations  worldwide  to  enhance  the  efficiency  of  localized  policy
implementation.

Our  findings,  supported  by  statistical,  econometric,  and  machine
learning analyses, offer new ways to capture and predict poverty pat-
terns.  Visual  summaries  in  this  study  illustrate  how  data  can  inform
policy design, guide the implementation of economic interventions, and
validate key relationships between poverty, inequality, and marginal-
ized  populations.  While  developed  within  a  Philippine  context,  these
tools  are  applicable  to  other  developing  regions  seeking  scalable,
evidence-based  solutions  for  economic  development  and  poverty
reduction.

Machine learning regression and classification models presented in
this work provide a replicable framework for enhancing poverty alle-
viation  strategies.  These  tools  support  data-informed  decision-making
and can improve the precision of program targeting. Once completed,
the full dataset and methodological documentation will be made avail-
able  to  assist  governments,  researchers,  and  development  agencies  in
replicating or adapting the approach for broader use.
