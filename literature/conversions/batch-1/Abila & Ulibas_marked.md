ABSTRACT  
This  study  investigates  the  financial  management  practices  and  financial  resilience  of  online 
freelancers in Laguna within the context of the newly implemented Digital Platform Taxation (DPT), 
which imposes a 12% VAT on digital services. Guided by Carroll’s Buffer Stock Theory, the study 
examines how Buffer Stock Savings (BSS), Perceived Income Volatility (PIV), and Liquid Asset 
Accessibility (LAA) influence freelancers’ capacity to withstand financial shocks. A mixed-methods 
exploratory pilot approach was employed. Quantitatively, a structured survey was administered to a 
purposive-stratified sample of 30 online freelancers. Qualitatively, semi-structured interviews were 
conducted with a criterion-purposive subsample of 10 participants to contextualize the statistical data, 
enriching  and  validating  the  survey  findings.  Descriptive  statistics,  Pearson’s  Correlation,  and 
Multiple Linear Regression were used to analyze the quantitative data, alongside thematic coding for 
qualitative responses. Results indicate moderate relationships among BSS, PIV, LAA, and overall 
financial  resilience,  supporting  the  relevance  of  Buffer  Stock  Theory  in  the  digital  gig  economy. 
Qualitative  insights  further  revealed  freelancers’  adaptive  financial  behaviors  and  the  challenges 
posed by the 12% VAT pressure. The study’s findings suggest that while freelancers exhibit adaptive 
strategies, their resilience is primarily short-term. The study supports the development of targeted 
financial literacy program aimed at strengthening the financial stability and planning capabilities of 
online freelancers in Laguna, with implications for policymakers and digital platforms.

INTRODUCTION

The expansion of the digital economy has significantly reshaped employment structures worldwide, particularly 
through  the  rise  of  online  freelancing.  Digital  platforms  such  as  Upwork,  OnlineJobs.ph,  Fiverr,  and  similar 
marketplaces have enabled individuals to offer services beyond geographic boundaries, creating flexible income 
opportunities. However, while freelancing offers autonomy and accessibility, it also exposes workers to income 
instability, lack of formal employment protection, and limited access to traditional financial safety nets. These 
conditions make financial management and resilience critical concerns for freelancers, especially in developing 
economies such as the Philippines where social protection systems are often employment-based.

Starting June 01, 2025, the Philippine government introduced Digital Platform Taxation (DPT) policies, including 
the imposition of value-added tax (VAT) on digital services and subscriptions used by freelancers. While these 
regulations aim to broaden the tax base and ensure fiscal sustainability, they have introduced additional financial 
pressure on freelancers whose earnings are already irregular. Platform fees, subscription costs, and compliance 
requirements  further  reduce  net  income,  intensifying  the  need  for  effective  financial  management  strategies. 
Despite the growing relevance of this issue, empirical research on how digital taxation affects freelancers’ financial 
resilience remains limited, particularly at the local or provincial level.

Existing  research  on  financial  resilience  has  largely  focused  on  salaried  workers,  households,  or  micro-
entrepreneurs, emphasizing savings behavior, access to liquid assets, and income stability as key determinants of 
resilience.  Buffer  Stock  Theory  suggests  that  individuals  facing  income  uncertainty  tend  to  accumulate 
precautionary  savings  to  smooth  consumption  during  adverse  shocks.  Related  studies  also  highlight  perceived 
income volatility as a psychological and behavioral factor influencing financial decision-making. However, there 
is a noticeable gap in the literature regarding how these concepts apply to online freelancers operating under digital 
platform taxation regimes, especially within the Philippine context.

This  study  was  conducted  to  address  this  gap  by  examining  the  financial  management  practices  and  financial 
resilience of online freelancers in Laguna amid the implementation of digital platform taxation. Specifically, it 
sought to determine the levels of buffer stock savings, perceived income volatility, and liquid asset accessibility 
among freelancers, and to analyze how these factors relate to financial resilience. The study was guided by the 
following research questions: (1) What are the actual financial behaviors of online freelancers in Laguna amid 
digital platform taxation, based on: Savings behavior and frequency, Instances of financial strain, Responses to 
hypothetical financial shock scenarios, and Voluntary submission of financial records? (2) What are the financial 
management practices of online freelancers in Laguna amid digital platform taxation in terms of the key variables 
of  the  Buffer  Stock  Savings  Theory:  Buffer  Stock  Savings,  Perceived  Income  Volatility,  and  Liquid  Asset 
Accessibility?  (3)  What  is  the  level  of  financial  resilience  among  online  freelancers  in  Laguna  amid  digital 
platform  taxation  in  terms  of  the  key  variables  of  the  Buffer  Stock  Savings  Theory:  Buffer  Stock  Savings, 
Perceived Income Volatility, and Liquid Asset Accessibility? (4) What financial literacy program can be proposed 
to enhance the financial management practices and financial resilience of online freelancers in Laguna within the 
context of digital platform taxation?

By addressing these questions, the study aims to contribute localized empirical evidence on freelance financial 
management  practices  and  resilience,  and  to  provide  a  foundation  for  practical  financial  literacy  interventions 
tailored to the realities of digitally taxed freelance work.

This study is limited to online freelancers in Laguna, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings to 
other  regions  or  countries.  The  sample  included  only  registered  online  freelancers  who  uses  prominent  online 
platforms and excluded other segments of the gig economy and traditional employment. As a cross-sectional study, 
it identified relationships between variables at one point in time and could not establish causal effects or long-term

resilience  outcomes.  For  instance,  while  a  'practice'  may  involve  the  frequency  of  saving  into  a  buffer,  the 
'resilience' component measures the perceived adequacy of that buffer to cover essential expenses during a sudden 
loss of income.

framework, focusing on measurable variables including Buffer Stock Savings, Perceived Income Volatility, Liquid 
Asset Accessibility, and Financial Resilience. Data were collected using a structured survey questionnaire, and 
quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Pearson’s correlation, and multiple linear regression

Ethical  considerations  were  strictly  observed  throughout  the  study  to  protect  participants’  rights  and  welfare. 
Participants were provided with informed consent information describing the study’s objectives, procedures, risks, 
and benefits. Participation was voluntary, and respondents were allowed to withdraw at any time without penalty.

2.2 Research Locale 
The research was conducted among online freelancers residing in Laguna, Philippines, a province recognized as a 
growing  hub  for  digital  professionals  within  the  CALABARZON  region.  The  area  was  selected  due  to  its 
developing digital infrastructure and active participation in the gig economy. The target population consisted of 
online freelancers engaged in project-based or task-based online work using platforms such as LinkedIn, Facebook 
freelancer  communities,  OnlineJobs.ph,  and  other  local  networks.  Based  on  preliminary  online  platform 
observations conducted in 2025, the estimated population of online freelancers in Laguna was 1,903 individuals.

2.4 Research Instruments 
The primary research instrument was a structured questionnaire composed of several sections measuring financial 
management practices, financial resilience, and perceptions of digital platform taxation. Financial management 
constructs were measured using 5-point semantic differential scales. Composite scores were generated to represent 
key variables for statistical analysis. A separate section measured financial resilience by assessing participants’ 
ability to recover from financial shocks. Perceptions of digital platform taxation were also measured to assess 
awareness of the taxation system, perceived effects on costs and take-home pay, and the extent to which VAT 
influenced their budgeting and financial security. Demographic information such as age, gender, freelancing niche, 
years of experience, and income bracket was also collected to support subgroup analysis.

To  ensure  instrument  validity  and  reliability,  the  questionnaire  underwent  expert  validation  from  a  freelance 
accounting  taxation  specialist  and  an  academic  expert  with  an  MBA  and  a  PhD  in  Information  Technology 
Management.  A  pilot  test  involving  15–30  freelancers  was  conducted  to  assess  internal  consistency  using 
Cronbach’s Alpha, with α ≥ 0.70 considered acceptable.

2.5 Data Gathering Procedure 
Data collection followed ethical and systematic procedures. Ethical clearance was obtained from the Graduate 
School  Research  Committee  prior  to  data  gathering.  Permission  was  also  requested  from  online  community 
administrators  before  distributing  surveys.  The  questionnaire  was  administered  through  an  online  platform  to 
efficiently reach geographically dispersed participants. Informed consent was required before respondents could 
access the survey. Data collection was conducted shortly from late September to October 2025. As this period was 
after the June 1, 2025 implementation of Digital Platform Taxation, the findings primarily reflect reactive financial 
behaviors among freelancers.

Collected data underwent cleaning and preparation processes to ensure completeness and accuracy. Responses 
were screened for missing data, inconsistencies, and outliers before statistical analysis. Data were stored securely 
in password-protected databases to maintain confidentiality and anonymity. After preparation, data were analyzed 
using SPSS software. Descriptive statistics such as mean, frequency, and standard deviation were used to describe 
sample characteristics and variable distributions. Pearson’s correlation was used to examine relationships among 
financial management variables and financial resilience, while multiple linear regression was used to determine 
predictive relationships.

2.6 Data Analysis Plan 
Qualitative data were analyzed using deductive thematic and content analysis based on predetermined categories 
including  BSS,  PIV,  LAA,  and  FR.  Illustrative  quotes  and  narrative  summaries  were  used  to  enrich  the 
interpretation of quantitative results. Qualitative prompts were pilot-tested for clarity and alignment with research 
objectives. The researcher also maintained reflexivity in interpreting responses. Triangulation through open-ended 
responses  and  documentary  evidence  enhanced  the  credibility  of  findings.  The  study  also  aimed  to  develop  a 
proposed financial literacy program based on combined qualitative and quantitative results to address identified 
financial challenges among freelancers.

Interpretation of results followed predefined scoring classifications. Financial management practices and resilience 
levels were categorized using mean score ranges to determine whether practices were very low, low, moderate, 
high,  or  very  high.  Composite  scores  were  treated  as  continuous  variables  for  parametric  testing  when 
distributional assumptions were satisfied; otherwise, nonparametric measures were applied.

Overall, the integration of quantitative measurement and qualitative contextualization strengthened the reliability, 
validity, and practical relevance of the study findings.

RESULTS

The results of this study provide a comprehensive understanding of the financial behaviors, management practices, 
and  resilience  of  online  freelancers  in  Laguna  within  the  context  of  digital  platform  taxation.  Using  a  mixed-
methods approach, quantitative and qualitative findings were integrated to explain how freelancers manage income 
uncertainty, taxation pressures, and financial risks associated with gig economy work.

3.1 Actual Financial Behaviors 
Findings revealed that online freelancers demonstrated diverse financial behavior patterns classified as cautious 
savers, adaptive spenders, and vulnerable freelancers. Most participants practiced adaptive financial management 
through  micro-saving  strategies,  primarily  using  digital  wallets  for  emergency  funds  and  short-term  liquidity 
needs. Savings behavior was largely conditional on client workload and income flow, reflecting reactive financial 
planning  rather  than  structured  long-term  saving  systems.  Many  freelancers  prioritized  essential  household 
expenses  over  savings,  especially  during  low-income  periods.  Income  instability  and  digital  platform  taxation

3.3 Level of Financial Resilience 
Results showed that freelancers had moderate financial resilience, with an overall mean score of 3.19 (SD = 1.30). 
Freelancers  expressed  confidence  in  recovering  from  financial  setbacks  (M  =  3.81)  but  showed  limitations  in 
managing long-term financial stocks such as medical emergencies (M = 2.85) or unexpected income loss (M = 
2.88). The highest resilience score was observed in self-confidence in financial recovery, while the lowest scores 
were related to medical expense preparedness and long-term emergency funding capacity. This indicates a gap 
between psychological confidence and actual financial preparedness.

4. Research Implications 
The findings of the study revealed several financial management challenges faced by freelancers, particularly in 
terms of savings discipline, financial planning, and tax compliance awareness. The results suggest that freelancers 
often  experience  income  volatility  and  financial  uncertainty,  which  may  affect  their  ability  to  maintain  stable 
financial practices. Moreover, the study highlights the need for improved financial literacy and accessible support 
systems that can help freelancers develop more sustainable financial behaviors. Based on these results and the 
conclusions drawn from the analysis, several recommendations are proposed to strengthen financial management 
practices  among  freelancers,  enhance  financial  literacy  programs,  and  encourage  institutional  support  from 
policymakers and financial institutions.

CONCLUSION

The study concluded that the financial behaviors of online freelancers are largely shaped by income variability and 
digital  platform  taxation.  Although  freelancers  demonstrated  adaptive  saving  and  liquidity  practices,  these 
strategies  were  mainly  reactive  and  focused  on  short-term  financial  survival  rather  than  long-term  financial 
security. 
Financial management practices were generally positive but uneven across respondents. Good saving and liquidity 
behaviors coexisted with moderate challenges in managing income volatility and gaps in tax knowledge. These 
findings suggest that financial discipline among freelancers is influenced by external economic pressures as well 
as individual financial literacy levels.

Financial  resilience  was  found  to  be  moderate.  Freelancers  generally  showed  confidence  in  recovering  from 
financial setbacks but were less prepared for large-scale financial shocks such as medical emergencies or prolonged 
income disruptions. Access to immediately available funds was identified as an important factor in supporting 
financial  stability.  Digital  platform  taxation  influenced  both  financial  stress  and  financial  awareness.  While 
taxation  reduced  disposable  income,  it  also  encouraged  freelancers  to  improve  recordkeeping  and  financial 
monitoring  practices.  Overall,  resilience  among  freelancers  was  shown  to  be  a  dynamic  outcome  shaped  by 
behavioral, economic, and structural factors. The study also supported the applicability of the Buffer Stock Saving 
Theory  in  explaining  freelancer  financial  behavior.  Freelancers  demonstrated  precautionary  saving  patterns  by 
maintaining short-term financial buffers, using easily accessible savings platforms, and adjusting saving habits 
based on income conditions and taxation pressures.

Recommendations 
Freelancers are encouraged to build structured savings and maintain accessible emergency funds while considering 
post-tax  income  and  platform  fees.  Adaptive  budgeting  techniques  that  account  for  irregular  earnings  remain 
essential, and the use of digital tools and e-wallets to monitor income, expenses, and liquidity in real time can help 
freelancers  respond  more  effectively  to  financial  shocks.  Automated  or  scheduled  savings  mechanisms  within 
mobile banking or e-wallet platforms are also recommended to reduce reliance on reactive saving behaviors and 
informal  borrowing.  Participation  in  peer  networks  or  cooperatives,  such  as  the  Filipino  Online  Professional 
Service Cooperative (FOPSco), may further support financial security through mentoring, shared resources, and 
cooperative-based savings programs.

Targeted interventions should also address gaps in financial management practices. Freelancers with low buffer 
stock  savings  should  receive  training  on  short-term  and  post-tax  savings  planning,  while  those  experiencing 
difficulty  managing  income  volatility  should  be  supported  through  scenario-based  budgeting  and  financial 
planning strategies. Similarly, freelancers with limited liquid asset accessibility should be guided in developing 
emergency funds, improving liquidity management, and diversifying income sources.

INTRODUCTION
The expansion of the digital economy has significantly reshaped employment structures worldwide, particularly
through the rise of online freelancing. Digital platforms such as Upwork, OnlineJobs.ph, Fiverr, and similar
marketplaces have enabled individuals to offer services beyond geographic boundaries, creating flexible income
opportunities. However, while freelancing offers autonomy and accessibility, it also exposes workers to income
instability, lack of formal employment protection, and limited access to traditional financial safety nets. These
conditions make financial management and resilience critical concerns for freelancers, especially in developing
economies such as the Philippines where social protection systems are often employment-based.
Starting June 01, 2025, the Philippine government introduced Digital Platform Taxation (DPT) policies, including
the imposition of value-added tax (VAT) on digital services and subscriptions used by freelancers. While these
regulations aim to broaden the tax base and ensure fiscal sustainability, they have introduced additional financial
pressure on freelancers whose earnings are already irregular. Platform fees, subscription costs, and compliance
requirements further reduce net income, intensifying the need for effective financial management strategies.
Despite the growing relevance of this issue, empirical research on how digital taxation affects freelancers’ financial
resilience remains limited, particularly at the local or provincial level.
Existing research on financial resilience has largely focused on salaried workers, households, or micro-
entrepreneurs, emphasizing savings behavior, access to liquid assets, and income stability as key determinants of
resilience. Buffer Stock Theory suggests that individuals facing income uncertainty tend to accumulate
precautionary savings to smooth consumption during adverse shocks. Related studies also highlight perceived
income volatility as a psychological and behavioral factor influencing financial decision-making. However, there
is a noticeable gap in the literature regarding how these concepts apply to online freelancers operating under digital
platform taxation regimes, especially within the Philippine context.
This study was conducted to address this gap by examining the financial management practices and financial
resilience of online freelancers in Laguna amid the implementation of digital platform taxation. Specifically, it
sought to determine the levels of buffer stock savings, perceived income volatility, and liquid asset accessibility
among freelancers, and to analyze how these factors relate to financial resilience. The study was guided by the
following research questions: (1) What are the actual financial behaviors of online freelancers in Laguna amid
digital platform taxation, based on: Savings behavior and frequency, Instances of financial strain, Responses to
hypothetical financial shock scenarios, and Voluntary submission of financial records? (2) What are the financial
management practices of online freelancers in Laguna amid digital platform taxation in terms of the key variables
of the Buffer Stock Savings Theory: Buffer Stock Savings, Perceived Income Volatility, and Liquid Asset
Accessibility? (3) What is the level of financial resilience among online freelancers in Laguna amid digital
platform taxation in terms of the key variables of the Buffer Stock Savings Theory: Buffer Stock Savings,
Perceived Income Volatility, and Liquid Asset Accessibility? (4) What financial literacy program can be proposed
to enhance the financial management practices and financial resilience of online freelancers in Laguna within the
context of digital platform taxation?
By addressing these questions, the study aims to contribute localized empirical evidence on freelance financial
management practices and resilience, and to provide a foundation for practical financial literacy interventions
tailored to the realities of digitally taxed freelance work.

other regions or countries. The sample included only registered online freelancers who uses prominent online
platforms and excluded other segments of the gig economy and traditional employment. As a cross-sectional study,
it identified relationships between variables at one point in time and could not establish causal effects or long-term

2.4 Research Instruments
The primary research instrument was a structured questionnaire composed of several sections measuring financial
management practices, financial resilience, and perceptions of digital platform taxation. Financial management
constructs were measured using 5-point semantic differential scales. Composite scores were generated to represent
key variables for statistical analysis. A separate section measured financial resilience by assessing participants’
ability to recover from financial shocks. Perceptions of digital platform taxation were also measured to assess
awareness of the taxation system, perceived effects on costs and take-home pay, and the extent to which VAT
influenced their budgeting and financial security. Demographic information such as age, gender, freelancing niche,
years of experience, and income bracket was also collected to support subgroup analysis.
To ensure instrument validity and reliability, the questionnaire underwent expert validation from a freelance
accounting taxation specialist and an academic expert with an MBA and a PhD in Information Technology
Management. A pilot test involving 15–30 freelancers was conducted to assess internal consistency using
Cronbach’s Alpha, with α ≥ 0.70 considered acceptable.

2.5 Data Gathering Procedure
Data collection followed ethical and systematic procedures. Ethical clearance was obtained from the Graduate
School Research Committee prior to data gathering. Permission was also requested from online community
administrators before distributing surveys. The questionnaire was administered through an online platform to
efficiently reach geographically dispersed participants. Informed consent was required before respondents could
access the survey. Data collection was conducted shortly from late September to October 2025. As this period was
after the June 1, 2025 implementation of Digital Platform Taxation, the findings primarily reflect reactive financial
behaviors among freelancers.
Collected data underwent cleaning and preparation processes to ensure completeness and accuracy. Responses
were screened for missing data, inconsistencies, and outliers before statistical analysis. Data were stored securely
in password-protected databases to maintain confidentiality and anonymity. After preparation, data were analyzed
using SPSS software. Descriptive statistics such as mean, frequency, and standard deviation were used to describe
sample characteristics and variable distributions. Pearson’s correlation was used to examine relationships among
financial management variables and financial resilience, while multiple linear regression was used to determine
predictive relationships.
2.6 Data Analysis Plan
Qualitative data were analyzed using deductive thematic and content analysis based on predetermined categories
including BSS, PIV, LAA, and FR. Illustrative quotes and narrative summaries were used to enrich the
interpretation of quantitative results. Qualitative prompts were pilot-tested for clarity and alignment with research
objectives. The researcher also maintained reflexivity in interpreting responses. Triangulation through open-ended
responses and documentary evidence enhanced the credibility of findings. The study also aimed to develop a
proposed financial literacy program based on combined qualitative and quantitative results to address identified
financial challenges among freelancers.
Interpretation of results followed predefined scoring classifications. Financial management practices and resilience
levels were categorized using mean score ranges to determine whether practices were very low, low, moderate,
high, or very high. Composite scores were treated as continuous variables for parametric testing when
distributional assumptions were satisfied; otherwise, nonparametric measures were applied.
Overall, the integration of quantitative measurement and qualitative contextualization strengthened the reliability,
validity, and practical relevance of the study findings.
RESULTS
The results of this study provide a comprehensive understanding of the financial behaviors, management practices,
and resilience of online freelancers in Laguna within the context of digital platform taxation. Using a mixed-
methods approach, quantitative and qualitative findings were integrated to explain how freelancers manage income
uncertainty, taxation pressures, and financial risks associated with gig economy work.

needs. Savings behavior was largely conditional on client workload and income flow, reflecting reactive financial
planning rather than structured long-term saving systems. Many freelancers prioritized essential household

4. Research Implications
The findings of the study revealed several financial management challenges faced by freelancers, particularly in
terms of savings discipline, financial planning, and tax compliance awareness. The results suggest that freelancers
often experience income volatility and financial uncertainty, which may affect their ability to maintain stable
financial practices. Moreover, the study highlights the need for improved financial literacy and accessible support
systems that can help freelancers develop more sustainable financial behaviors. Based on these results and the
conclusions drawn from the analysis, several recommendations are proposed to strengthen financial management
practices among freelancers, enhance financial literacy programs, and encourage institutional support from
policymakers and financial institutions.
CONCLUSION
The study concluded that the financial behaviors of online freelancers are largely shaped by income variability and
digital platform taxation. Although freelancers demonstrated adaptive saving and liquidity practices, these
strategies were mainly reactive and focused on short-term financial survival rather than long-term financial
security.
Financial management practices were generally positive but uneven across respondents. Good saving and liquidity
behaviors coexisted with moderate challenges in managing income volatility and gaps in tax knowledge. These
findings suggest that financial discipline among freelancers is influenced by external economic pressures as well
as individual financial literacy levels.
Financial resilience was found to be moderate. Freelancers generally showed confidence in recovering from
financial setbacks but were less prepared for large-scale financial shocks such as medical emergencies or prolonged
income disruptions. Access to immediately available funds was identified as an important factor in supporting
financial stability. Digital platform taxation influenced both financial stress and financial awareness. While
taxation reduced disposable income, it also encouraged freelancers to improve recordkeeping and financial
monitoring practices. Overall, resilience among freelancers was shown to be a dynamic outcome shaped by
behavioral, economic, and structural factors. The study also supported the applicability of the Buffer Stock Saving
Theory in explaining freelancer financial behavior. Freelancers demonstrated precautionary saving patterns by
maintaining short-term financial buffers, using easily accessible savings platforms, and adjusting saving habits
based on income conditions and taxation pressures.
Recommendations
Freelancers are encouraged to build structured savings and maintain accessible emergency funds while considering
post-tax income and platform fees. Adaptive budgeting techniques that account for irregular earnings remain
essential, and the use of digital tools and e-wallets to monitor income, expenses, and liquidity in real time can help
freelancers respond more effectively to financial shocks. Automated or scheduled savings mechanisms within
mobile banking or e-wallet platforms are also recommended to reduce reliance on reactive saving behaviors and
informal borrowing. Participation in peer networks or cooperatives, such as the Filipino Online Professional
Service Cooperative (FOPSco), may further support financial security through mentoring, shared resources, and
cooperative-based savings programs.
Targeted interventions should also address gaps in financial management practices. Freelancers with low buffer
stock savings should receive training on short-term and post-tax savings planning, while those experiencing
difficulty managing income volatility should be supported through scenario-based budgeting and financial
planning strategies. Similarly, freelancers with limited liquid asset accessibility should be guided in developing
emergency funds, improving liquidity management, and diversifying income sources.
