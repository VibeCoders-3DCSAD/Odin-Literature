ABSTRACT 
Understanding  the  relationship  of  financial  well-being  to  years  of  investing  and  income  is 
essential  in  achieving  personal  financial  success  and  laying  a  solid  foundation  for  financial 
stability, as well as how young professionals can attain an improved state of financial well-
being.  In  this  study,  the  researchers  intended  to  determine  whether  years  of  investing  and 
income  correlate  with  financial  well-being  of  young  professionals  in  the  National  Capital 
Region. Using a descriptive-correlational research design, the researchers examined the data 
collected  from  385  young  professionals  aged  25-35.  Findings  showed  that  the  years  of 
investing were considerably low but positively correlated to financial well-being. The findings 
also  suggested  that  while  income  is  a  factor  in  financial  well-being,  it  is  not  the  sole 
determinant. The findings from the research suggest a need for targeted investment education 
programs and promotion of financial well-being in initiatives to enhance young professionals' 
financial well-being.

Keywords: Financial Well-being; Years of Investing; Income; Young Professionals.

1. INTRODUCTION

Among young professionals, investments in trading like stocks, life and health insurance, and 
retirement plans are of paramount importance. At the same time, individual short and long-
term goals and preferences are considered. According to a Manulife survey (n.d.), managing 
finances,  financial  independence,  and  even  starting  a  business  were  among  the  top  five 
priorities  identified  by  500  Gen  Z  and  Millennials.  Young  professionals  increasingly  seek 
financial  independence,  which  is  reflected  in  purchasing  decisions  to  support  goods  and 
services whose future is warranted and corresponds to their beliefs.

Although studies on financial well-being keep progressing, there still is a gap in the 
research  on  how  income,  years  of  investing,  and  financial  well-being  influence  each  other,

Despite the amount of existing literature that covers diverse aspects of financial literacy and

The study aims to answer the following research questions:

professionals in the National Capital Region? 
3. What is the relationship between financial well-being and income of young professionals in

Studying the dependence of financial well-being to years of investing and income may 
enable individuals to make informed financial decisions, promote financial security, improve 
personal financial management skills and guide policy interventions. The findings can offer 
insights  for  understanding  similar  groups  in  other  emerging  economies  facing  comparable 
challenges, urbanization, job markets, and accessibility to financial products. The influence of 
income on well-being observed here might reflect a universal need for financial well-being to

broader benefits of long-term financial planning and the accumulation of financial knowledge 
that can lead to increased confidence and reduced stress among young professionals in diverse 
markets (Lusardi & Mitchell, 2014). Therefore, while this study is anchored in the Philippine 
context, it aims to contribute to a broader understanding of the financial determinants of young 
professional well-being which offers a perspective through which other nations and sectors can 
examine their own dynamics.

2. REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE

Financial well-being means having enough money to live comfortably (Lambert et al., 2023). 
It is determined by how an individual control their finances, ability to endure financial stress, 
meet his or her financial goals, and have financial freedom (Consumer Financial Protection 
Bureau, n.d.).  It also involves control over their financial situation and making choices that 
align with their values and goals without undue financial constraints (Sabri et al., 2023).

As  explained  by  Erner  et  al.  (2016),  the  assessment  of  financial  well-being  can  be 
objective and subjective. The study indicates that objective financial well-being is quantifiable 
and  emphasizes  factors  like  credit  scores,  financial  capability,  and  the  ability  to  handle 
unexpected  expenses.  On  the  other  hand,  subjective  financial  well-being  is  often  based  on 
personal  perceptions  and  feelings  of  financial  security  and  control.  In  comparable  financial 
situations, people's subjective financial well-being may differ (She et al., 2022).

factors of the young professionals’ well-being, a study shows that financial behavior, financial 
literacy, personal traits,  social and economic environment,  and life stage  contribute to  their 
financial  well-being,  interpreting  that  this  will  impact  their  financial  management  skills 
(Lambert et al., 2023).

2.2 Years of Investing to Financial Well-being 
The years of investing may impact their investment decisions. This is supported by the study 
of Lin et al. (2022), the results showed that most of the investors aged 18 to 54 years old, who 
have less than two years to less than ten years of experience in investing, are likely to engage 
in various investment vehicles, specifically in bonds, stocks, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), 
mutual funds, or other securities outside of retirement accounts.

Moreover,  Collins  and  Urban  (2019)  had  this  to  say  that  when  individuals  begin 
investing at a younger age, they have the advantage of time in the number of years of investing 
in relation to financial well-being. When they start early in life, their investments have a long 
period to grow and compound, allowing them to withstand market fluctuations, take advantage 
of  compounding  returns,  and  accumulate  wealth  over  the  years.  As  a  result,  they  may 
experience positive financial well-being later in life compared to those who start investing later.

3. THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

The relationship among the variables of this study is grounded on three theories, i.e., Subjective 
Well-being Theory, Life-Course Theory, and Theory of Planned Behavior. The first theory was 
Conceptualized  by  Ed  Diener,  who  claimed  that  subjective  well-being  refers  to  a  person’s 
overall satisfaction with their life and their emotional well-being, emphasizing the importance 
of life satisfaction in assessing well-being. Research made by Carton et al (2022) from social 
housing  tenants  in  Ireland  has  shown  that  financial  well-being  is  positively  correlated  with 
subjective well-being, indicating that individuals who have better financial well-being also tend 
to have higher levels of subjective well-being.

Elder  (1998),  who  is  known  for  his  work  on  Life-Course  Theory,  emphasized  the 
importance of understanding how experience and events throughout a person’s life can shape 
their  development  and  well-being.  According  to  Cheung  (2015),  income  and  investing  are 
important factors in life course theory because it can have an impact on an individual’s well-
being. The theory emphasizes the importance of income in understanding how social, economic 
and historical factors shape individuals’ life over time, therefore showing income as one of the 
key factors that influence these paths. In terms of investments, life course theory suggests that 
individuals’ investment decisions and strategies may vary depending on their stage of life and 
financial  goals.  As  highlighted  by  Collins  (2019),  life-course  theory  suggests  that  financial 
well-being is influenced by various factors, including early life experiences, accumulation of 
financial  resources,  and  social  mobility.  For  instance,  individuals  who  start  investing  at  a 
younger age may have a longer time to accumulate wealth, which can lead to greater financial 
well-being later in life. In the same manner, individuals with higher incomes may have more 
resources to invest, which can also lead to greater financial well-being.

Conceptualized  by  Icek  Ajzen,  the  Theory  of  Planned  Behavior  suggests  that  an 
individual’s behavior is determined by their intention to perform a pattern, which is influenced 
by their attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control (Lambert et al, 2023). A 
favorable attitude towards investing may arise from beliefs about the benefits of investing, of 
which some of them are, wealth accumulation, financial security, or achieving long-term goals 
(Sinnewe  &  Nicholson,  2023).  When  applied  to  financial  decision-making  and  investing, 
subjective  norms  influence  individuals’  behavior  because  they  are  socially  expected  or 
approved in the context of financial behaviors (Sun et al, 2018). If individuals are aware and 
conscious  that  they  have  the  necessary  resources,  skills,  and  investment  knowledge  to 
participate in investment activities, they are more likely to have a positive perceived behavioral 
control and likelihood to invest (Iramani and Lufti, 2021; de Castro et al., 2020). In the context 
of  income,  it  can  affect  an  individual’s  subjective  norms  around  financial  management  as 
expectations and pressures in society differ based on income level (She et al., 2022).

5. METHODS

This study was based on the survey designed to examine the causal relationship between years 
of investing and income, and financial well-being among young professionals in the National 
Capital Region. The quantitative approach was used to achieve the research objectives.

The questionnaire, was adopted from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (n.d.) 
entitled “Measuring financial well-being: A guide to  using the CFPB Financial  Well-Being 
Scale.” The authors of this adopted questionnaire gave permission to the public who will use 
it, especially for researchers or practitioners. The resulting survey instrument for the current 
study  was  validated  by  academic  experts  who  assessed  the  nature  and  complexity  of  the 
questions. Afterwards, the survey instrument was pilot tested, yielding a Cronbach Alpha of 
0.964, which is considered reliable compared to similar studies. The first part of the instrument 
was focused on the selection criteria questions which must all be answered in the affirmative, 
namely, 1.) whether they are 25-35 years old, 2.) if they are working and residing in NCR, and 
3.) if they are investing. The second part focused on getting the respondents’ basic information 
such as age, gender, civil status, highest educational attainment, monthly income, number of 
years of investing, and where they work in NCR. The third or main section of the questionnaire 
determined the level of the financial well-being of the young professionals in NCR. This main 
section consists of 18 questions, answerable with the use of a 4-point Likert scale, denoting 
“Strongly Agree” to “Strongly Disagree.” The interpretation of the results in the 4-point Likert 
scale was based on the article of Pimentel (2019), where a 3.51-4.00 interval is the highest,

6.3.1 The level of financial well-being of young professionals in NCR 
Results show that young professionals in NCR have sufficient levels of financial well-being. 
Respondents  demonstrate  moderate  financial  stability  and  can  manage  spending,  savings, 
investments,  setbacks,  and  unplanned  costs.  This  is  in  line  with  the  findings  of  Azul  et  al 
(2023),  where  young  professionals  can  achieve  good  financial  well-being  by  effectively

6.3.2 The level of financial well-being’s relationship on years of investing 
Findings show that the level of financial well-being to years of investing is significantly low 
but  significantly  correlated.    This  is  in  agreement  with  the  findings  of  Hu  et  al.  (2021)  on 
subjective well-being that there is a significant correlation between years of investing and an 
individual’s financial well-being. According to a BSP Annual Report (2023), the effect of the 
pandemic has reached a realization of saving and investing for circumstances like the recent 
contagion.  This  is  also  consistent  with  the  conclusions  of  Cheung  (2016)  emphasizing  the 
importance of income in understanding historical factors that shape the individual’s life over 
time. This is where the Theory of Planned Behavior made by Lambert et al (2023) comes into 
play. Respondent’s subjective norms involve social pressure on engaging in a behavior, which 
could include the encouragement of friends, family, or influencers regarding investing. Their 
perceived behavioral  control refers to putting the first two factors into action, which in this 
context would be the investor's confidence in deciding to invest, thereby learning to value their 
financial well-being and practice appropriate financial planning.

6.3.2 The level of financial well-being’s relationship on income 
Findings indicate that there is a low but significant correlation between financial well-being 
and income. This agrees with the findings of Mahdzan et al (2020) and Prakash et al (2022), 
emphasizing that the relationship of income and financial well-being are directly proportional. 
The study conducted by Burcher (2017) showed that financial well-being is not fully reflecting 
an individual’s income but is more of the reflection of the financial capability and expectation 
for success. Moreover, the findings is also in line with Yu and Chen's (2016) discussion on 
subjective  well-being  where  absolute  argument  states  that  income  helps  individuals  meet 
certain needs, in turn, directly contributing to their satisfaction, happiness and financial well-
being.  Indeed, income can influence the financial well-being of young professionals. Higher-
income levels generally contribute positively to financial well-being by providing individuals 
with more resources to meet their financial needs, save for the future, and achieve their desired 
lifestyle (Rahman et  al., 2021).  However, some  studies use the demographic profile, which 
comprises the income level, in testing for the influence on financial well-being.

7. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

7.1 Conclusions 
The  study  found  significant  low  correlations  of  financial  well-being  to  years  of  investing, 
income,  education,  and  financial  behavior.  Given  more  income,  longer  investment  years, 
higher education level, and sound financial behavior, a possible increase of one's financial well-
being  may  exist. People  with  higher  incomes,  and  level  of  education  bring  higher  financial 
well-being  and  can  find  more  investment  opportunities  in  longer  years  if  they  are  not  risk 
averse. Moreover, investing is also longer for financially and emotionally stable people than 
those  struggling  financially.  Also,  people  with  a  higher  income  can  put  money  into  many

The  observed  relationship  between  income  and  well-being  reflects  a  fundamental

stress  and  enhancing  overall  well-being  (Mbonigaba,  2021).  Similarly,  in  markets  where

impact of investing years compared to a less developed market with limited investment options. 
Additionally,  industry-specific  factors  such  as  job  security,  benefits  packages,  and 
compensation structures can influence the relative importance of well-being. (To et al., 2020) 
Furthermore,  future  research  could  test  the  generalizability  of  these  findings  by 
conducting  comparative  studies  across  different  countries,  industries  and  markets.  It  is 
important  to  note  the  economic  development  levels,  compensation  models  and  regulatory 
policies  in  investments.  Such  studies  would  provide  a  more  nuanced  understanding  of  how 
financial factors universally and differentially impact young professional well-being that will 
allow for more tailored policy recommendations and financial literacy programs globally.

7.2 Recommendations 
Young professionals who have already started investing should continue. Consequently, they 
shall endeavor to develop a broader-based investment horizon, realizing that money invested 
generates income and investment job opportunities in the future. Consequently, this enhances 
the chances of them consistently having sustainable financial outcomes since they can make 
strategic investment choices that are in tandem with their long-term financial objectives. Also, 
these variables enable advisors to assist their clients in making choices about investments that 
will conveniently support their financial stability and deliver results that match their goals.

Analyzed results can be critical to leaders and financial professionals, and they should 
use them to invent precise measures to solve the financial situation of young professionals. The 
findings of the study could offer business and policy proposals (Mendoza et al., 2023) to further 
boost the well-being of young investors in the country. They could offer policy makers insights 
(Mandigma  and  Badoc-Gonzales,  2022)  on  how  a  stronger  financial  platform  that  leads  to 
economic welfare can be achieved by instigating programs that aid income growth and sustain 
financial involvement from investors.

Future researchers may consider integrating triangulation methods, where researchers 
can  conduct  both  qualitative  and  quantitative  research.  Thus,  researchers  can  gain  more 
comprehensive information regarding what influences people to use, which investment forms 
they use, and how the choices affect their financial literacy and well-being. Future researchers 
may opt for a larger sample size to enhance the study results' reliability and unpredictability

7.3 Implications 
In the light of the correlation and dependence of financial well-being to years of investment 
and income, the provision of targeted investment education programs is of no doubt. These 
plans should seek to do not just that but also to improve the knowledgeability of individuals 
with regard to how longer investment and income can affect one’s financial well-being and 
also, various investment strategies and the long-term gains that are achieved from investing. 
Since  the  academe  is  a  rich  ground  for  professionals  who  are  trained  to  participate  in 
interdisciplinary teams on functional areas (Mandigma, 2017), they can create an environment 
and a chance of better financial decisions through raising financial education and by intending 
the participants to act responsibly.

Financial well-being, investing years, and income draws the importance of enhancing 
individuals' financial state. The present study recognizes that financial information is important 
in these relations and functions (Mandigma, et al., 2016). Government and financial institutions 
will need to have special projects with the intent to develop people and help them in being 
financially stable. Offering those ways to access affordable financial products, budgeting and 
saving tools and debt management strategies would be a perfect example. The provision of the 
mechanisms  that  deal  with  financial  problems  and  create  a  favorable  environment  makes 
individuals feel more confident and participate in investing.

ABSTRACT
Understanding the relationship of financial well-being to years of investing and income is
essential in achieving personal financial success and laying a solid foundation for financial
stability, as well as how young professionals can attain an improved state of financial well-
being. In this study, the researchers intended to determine whether years of investing and
income correlate with financial well-being of young professionals in the National Capital
Region. Using a descriptive-correlational research design, the researchers examined the data
collected from 385 young professionals aged 25-35. Findings showed that the years of
investing were considerably low but positively correlated to financial well-being. The findings
also suggested that while income is a factor in financial well-being, it is not the sole
determinant. The findings from the research suggest a need for targeted investment education
programs and promotion of financial well-being in initiatives to enhance young professionals'
financial well-being.
Keywords: Financial Well-being; Years of Investing; Income; Young Professionals.

Among young professionals, investments in trading like stocks, life and health insurance, and

term goals and preferences are considered. According to a Manulife survey (n.d.), managing
finances, financial independence, and even starting a business were among the top five
priorities identified by 500 Gen Z and Millennials. Young professionals increasingly seek
financial independence, which is reflected in purchasing decisions to support goods and
services whose future is warranted and corresponds to their beliefs.
Although studies on financial well-being keep progressing, there still is a gap in the
research on how income, years of investing, and financial well-being influence each other,

Despite the amount of existing literature that covers diverse aspects of financial literacy and

2. REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE
Financial well-being means having enough money to live comfortably (Lambert et al., 2023).
It is determined by how an individual control their finances, ability to endure financial stress,
meet his or her financial goals, and have financial freedom (Consumer Financial Protection
Bureau, n.d.). It also involves control over their financial situation and making choices that
align with their values and goals without undue financial constraints (Sabri et al., 2023).
As explained by Erner et al. (2016), the assessment of financial well-being can be
objective and subjective. The study indicates that objective financial well-being is quantifiable
and emphasizes factors like credit scores, financial capability, and the ability to handle
unexpected expenses. On the other hand, subjective financial well-being is often based on
personal perceptions and feelings of financial security and control. In comparable financial
situations, people's subjective financial well-being may differ (She et al., 2022).

Modern society has improved financial well-being for young people because they can have a
smart and fulfilled life (Manalo et al., 2023). Nevertheless, studies indicate that the setbacks
they face on the way to financial independence are altogether different from those which
seniors and adults do (She et al., 2022). The same author identified two key categories

factors of the young professionals’ well-being, a study shows that financial behavior, financial
literacy, personal traits, social and economic environment, and life stage contribute to their
financial well-being, interpreting that this will impact their financial management skills
(Lambert et al., 2023).
2.2 Years of Investing to Financial Well-being
The years of investing may impact their investment decisions. This is supported by the study
of Lin et al. (2022), the results showed that most of the investors aged 18 to 54 years old, who
have less than two years to less than ten years of experience in investing, are likely to engage
in various investment vehicles, specifically in bonds, stocks, exchange-traded funds (ETFs),
mutual funds, or other securities outside of retirement accounts.
Moreover, Collins and Urban (2019) had this to say that when individuals begin
investing at a younger age, they have the advantage of time in the number of years of investing
in relation to financial well-being. When they start early in life, their investments have a long
period to grow and compound, allowing them to withstand market fluctuations, take advantage
of compounding returns, and accumulate wealth over the years. As a result, they may
experience positive financial well-being later in life compared to those who start investing later.

3. THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK
The relationship among the variables of this study is grounded on three theories, i.e., Subjective
Well-being Theory, Life-Course Theory, and Theory of Planned Behavior. The first theory was
Conceptualized by Ed Diener, who claimed that subjective well-being refers to a person’s
overall satisfaction with their life and their emotional well-being, emphasizing the importance
of life satisfaction in assessing well-being. Research made by Carton et al (2022) from social
housing tenants in Ireland has shown that financial well-being is positively correlated with
subjective well-being, indicating that individuals who have better financial well-being also tend
to have higher levels of subjective well-being.
Elder (1998), who is known for his work on Life-Course Theory, emphasized the
importance of understanding how experience and events throughout a person’s life can shape
their development and well-being. According to Cheung (2015), income and investing are
important factors in life course theory because it can have an impact on an individual’s well-
being. The theory emphasizes the importance of income in understanding how social, economic
and historical factors shape individuals’ life over time, therefore showing income as one of the
key factors that influence these paths. In terms of investments, life course theory suggests that
individuals’ investment decisions and strategies may vary depending on their stage of life and
financial goals. As highlighted by Collins (2019), life-course theory suggests that financial
well-being is influenced by various factors, including early life experiences, accumulation of
financial resources, and social mobility. For instance, individuals who start investing at a
younger age may have a longer time to accumulate wealth, which can lead to greater financial
well-being later in life. In the same manner, individuals with higher incomes may have more
resources to invest, which can also lead to greater financial well-being.
Conceptualized by Icek Ajzen, the Theory of Planned Behavior suggests that an
individual’s behavior is determined by their intention to perform a pattern, which is influenced
by their attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control (Lambert et al, 2023). A
favorable attitude towards investing may arise from beliefs about the benefits of investing, of
which some of them are, wealth accumulation, financial security, or achieving long-term goals
(Sinnewe & Nicholson, 2023). When applied to financial decision-making and investing,
subjective norms influence individuals’ behavior because they are socially expected or
approved in the context of financial behaviors (Sun et al, 2018). If individuals are aware and
conscious that they have the necessary resources, skills, and investment knowledge to
participate in investment activities, they are more likely to have a positive perceived behavioral
control and likelihood to invest (Iramani and Lufti, 2021; de Castro et al., 2020). In the context
of income, it can affect an individual’s subjective norms around financial management as
expectations and pressures in society differ based on income level (She et al., 2022).

5. METHODS
This study was based on the survey designed to examine the causal relationship between years
of investing and income, and financial well-being among young professionals in the National
Capital Region. The quantitative approach was used to achieve the research objectives.
The questionnaire, was adopted from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (n.d.)
entitled “Measuring financial well-being: A guide to using the CFPB Financial Well-Being
Scale.” The authors of this adopted questionnaire gave permission to the public who will use
it, especially for researchers or practitioners. The resulting survey instrument for the current
study was validated by academic experts who assessed the nature and complexity of the
questions. Afterwards, the survey instrument was pilot tested, yielding a Cronbach Alpha of
0.964, which is considered reliable compared to similar studies. The first part of the instrument
was focused on the selection criteria questions which must all be answered in the affirmative,
namely, 1.) whether they are 25-35 years old, 2.) if they are working and residing in NCR, and
3.) if they are investing. The second part focused on getting the respondents’ basic information
such as age, gender, civil status, highest educational attainment, monthly income, number of
years of investing, and where they work in NCR. The third or main section of the questionnaire
determined the level of the financial well-being of the young professionals in NCR. This main
section consists of 18 questions, answerable with the use of a 4-point Likert scale, denoting
“Strongly Agree” to “Strongly Disagree.” The interpretation of the results in the 4-point Likert
scale was based on the article of Pimentel (2019), where a 3.51-4.00 interval is the highest,

6.3 Discussion

6.3.1 The level of financial well-being of young professionals in NCR
Results show that young professionals in NCR have sufficient levels of financial well-being.
Respondents demonstrate moderate financial stability and can manage spending, savings,
investments, setbacks, and unplanned costs. This is in line with the findings of Azul et al
(2023), where young professionals  can achieve good financial well-being  by effectively
managing their money, living within their budget, and successfully addressing any financial
Copyright © 2026 GMP Press and Printing
ISSN: 2304-1013 (Online); 2304-1269 (CDROM); 2414-6722 (Print)

Review of Integrative Business and Economics Research, Vol. 15, Issue 2 912
challenges they can encounter. Moreover, respondents have a slightly confident attitude in their
financial objectives, income management, and security. Based on responses, knowing how to
prioritize, plan for challenges, and achieve financial stability can lead to future
satisfaction. This validates Elder’s Life Course Theory, where it is used to understand how
adolescence is connected to earlier development and life events in relation to situations that are
linked to later health and well-being. In the study of She et al. (2021), professional adults are
considered to have a positive financial attitude and the ability to manage finances, enhancing
their financial well-being. This suggests that having financial control is more likely to exhibit
financial well-being among young professionals.
6.3.2 The level of financial well-being’s relationship on years of investing
Findings show that the level of financial well-being to years of investing is significantly low
but significantly correlated. This is in agreement with the findings of Hu et al. (2021) on
subjective well-being that there is a significant correlation between years of investing and an
individual’s financial well-being. According to a BSP Annual Report (2023), the effect of the
pandemic has reached a realization of saving and investing for circumstances like the recent
contagion. This is also consistent with the conclusions of Cheung (2016) emphasizing the
importance of income in understanding historical factors that shape the individual’s life over
time. This is where the Theory of Planned Behavior made by Lambert et al (2023) comes into
play. Respondent’s subjective norms involve social pressure on engaging in a behavior, which
could include the encouragement of friends, family, or influencers regarding investing. Their
perceived behavioral control refers to putting the first two factors into action, which in this
context would be the investor's confidence in deciding to invest, thereby learning to value their
financial well-being and practice appropriate financial planning.
6.3.2 The level of financial well-being’s relationship on income
Findings indicate that there is a low but significant correlation between financial well-being
and income. This agrees with the findings of Mahdzan et al (2020) and Prakash et al (2022),
emphasizing that the relationship of income and financial well-being are directly proportional.
The study conducted by Burcher (2017) showed that financial well-being is not fully reflecting
an individual’s income but is more of the reflection of the financial capability and expectation
for success. Moreover, the findings is also in line with Yu and Chen's (2016) discussion on
subjective well-being where absolute argument states that income helps individuals meet
certain needs, in turn, directly contributing to their satisfaction, happiness and financial well-
being. Indeed, income can influence the financial well-being of young professionals. Higher-
income levels generally contribute positively to financial well-being by providing individuals
with more resources to meet their financial needs, save for the future, and achieve their desired
lifestyle (Rahman et al., 2021). However, some studies use the demographic profile, which
comprises the income level, in testing for the influence on financial well-being.
7. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
7.1 Conclusions
The study found significant low correlations of financial well-being to years of investing,
income, education, and financial behavior. Given more income, longer investment years,
higher education level, and sound financial behavior, a possible increase of one's financial well-
being may exist. People with higher incomes, and level of education bring higher financial
well-being and can find more investment opportunities in longer years if they are not risk
averse. Moreover, investing is also longer for financially and emotionally stable people than
those struggling financially. Also, people with a higher income can put money into many

strategies that work closely with their financial goals.
The observed relationship between income and well-being reflects a fundamental
human need for financial resources to ensure security and pursue life goals. This dynamic is
likely to be present in other developing nations experiencing economic growth and
urbanization. In countries with similar socio-economic structures to the Philippines, a
comparable study might yield analogous results regarding income's impact on young
professionals’ well-being, though the threshold for significant impact might differ based on
cost of living and cultural expectations. Even in developed economies, the relative impact of
income increases on well-being could show similarities particularly for young professionals
early in their careers facing housing costs or student loan debts, as seen in urban centers in the
United States (Simonse et al., 2024)
Further, the influence of investing years on well-being also gives relevance across
different markets. In industries with high income volatility, such as the gig economy, the ability
to leverage investment for long-term financial stability could be a crucial factor in mitigating
stress and enhancing overall well-being (Mbonigaba, 2021). Similarly, in markets where
pension systems are less robust, retirement planning falls more heavily on individual
investment which makes the experience gained over years of investing which is a predictor of
well-being among young professionals concerned with their financial future, (Lusardi &
Mitchell, 2014) A highly regulated and accessible market like Singapore might see a stronger
impact of investing years compared to a less developed market with limited investment options.
Additionally, industry-specific factors such as job security, benefits packages, and
compensation structures can influence the relative importance of well-being. (To et al., 2020)
Furthermore, future research could test the generalizability of these findings by
conducting comparative studies across different countries, industries and markets. It is
important to note the economic development levels, compensation models and regulatory
policies in investments. Such studies would provide a more nuanced understanding of how
financial factors universally and differentially impact young professional well-being that will
allow for more tailored policy recommendations and financial literacy programs globally.
7.2 Recommendations
Young professionals who have already started investing should continue. Consequently, they
shall endeavor to develop a broader-based investment horizon, realizing that money invested
generates income and investment job opportunities in the future. Consequently, this enhances
the chances of them consistently having sustainable financial outcomes since they can make
strategic investment choices that are in tandem with their long-term financial objectives. Also,
these variables enable advisors to assist their clients in making choices about investments that
will conveniently support their financial stability and deliver results that match their goals.
Analyzed results can be critical to leaders and financial professionals, and they should
use them to invent precise measures to solve the financial situation of young professionals. The
findings of the study could offer business and policy proposals (Mendoza et al., 2023) to further
boost the well-being of young investors in the country. They could offer policy makers insights
(Mandigma and Badoc-Gonzales, 2022) on how a stronger financial platform that leads to
economic welfare can be achieved by instigating programs that aid income growth and sustain
financial involvement from investors.
Future researchers may consider integrating triangulation methods, where researchers
can conduct both qualitative and quantitative research. Thus, researchers can gain more
comprehensive information regarding what influences people to use, which investment forms
they use, and how the choices affect their financial literacy and well-being. Future researchers
may opt for a larger sample size to enhance the study results' reliability and unpredictability

7.3 Implications
In the light of the correlation and dependence of financial well-being to years of investment
and income, the provision of targeted investment education programs is of no doubt. These
plans should seek to do not just that but also to improve the knowledgeability of individuals
with regard to how longer investment and income can affect one’s financial well-being and
also, various investment strategies and the long-term gains that are achieved from investing.
Since the academe is a rich ground for professionals who are trained to participate in
interdisciplinary teams on functional areas (Mandigma, 2017), they can create an environment
and a chance of better financial decisions through raising financial education and by intending
the participants to act responsibly.
Financial well-being, investing years, and income draws the importance of enhancing
individuals' financial state. The present study recognizes that financial information is important
in these relations and functions (Mandigma, et al., 2016). Government and financial institutions
will need to have special projects with the intent to develop people and help them in being
financially stable. Offering those ways to access affordable financial products, budgeting and
saving tools and debt management strategies would be a perfect example. The provision of the
mechanisms that deal with financial problems and create a favorable environment makes
individuals feel more confident and participate in investing.
